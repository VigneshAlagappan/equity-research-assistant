Signals investigations run a hypothesis-driven research pipeline, not a single-pass answer. Generate — several competing, falsifiable hypotheses are proposed before any evidence is gathered, spanning financial, operational, competitive, strategic, management, regulatory, macro, and industry angles, each broken into a short causal chain (cause → observed effect). Gather & evaluate — each hypothesis is checked independently against retrieved evidence (financial facts, filings, knowledge-graph relationships), with up to one additional targeted retrieval pass if the first evaluation reports a specific evidence gap. Synthesize — hypotheses are ranked and the strongest explanation is written up, alongside what remains unanswered.

  • Supported / Partially supported / Refuted / Insufficient evidence is an independent verdict per hypothesis, not one overall confidence score for the investigation.
  • The Leading / Contested / Weak / Unscored label and percentage on each hypothesis is the evidence strength the evaluator found for that hypothesis's verdict: Leading is 60% or higher, Contested is 30–59%, Weak is below 30%, and Unscored means no estimate was returned. It is not a probability the hypothesis is true, and scores aren't comparable across different investigations, since each one's evidence base differs.
  • The causal chain states how the hypothesis claims things would work, generated before any evidence was gathered — not a verified sequence of events. Whether it actually held is what the verdict and evidence assessment address.
