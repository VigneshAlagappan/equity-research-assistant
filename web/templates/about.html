About Signals

A personal research desk, not a data terminal.

Signals consolidates a company's financials, filings, and commentary into one place, and answers questions against that evidence instead of a headline. It's built to behave like a research analyst working for you — reading the primary sources, showing its work, and telling you plainly when it's guessing.

What it does

Every number on this site traces back to a source document and a reconciliation decision — annual reports, exchange filings, and licensed data exports, cross-checked against each other rather than taken on faith from a single vendor. On top of that layer sits a research assistant you can ask real questions: analyze a company over ten years, compare two companies, explain why a margin moved, or trace how a macro shift might show up in a specific balance sheet.

Answers are labeled, not just written. Every claim the assistant makes is tagged FACT, CALCULATION, or INFERENCE — so it's always clear what came straight from a filing, what was derived from it, and where the model is reasoning beyond the data. That distinction is the point of the product, not a footnote.

An investment philosophy

Signals exists because of a simple belief: good investing decisions come from clarity, not conviction borrowed from someone else's headline. Two kinds of clarity, specifically.

Thinking clarity

Form the view yourself, from primary sources, before letting anyone else's opinion into the room. Noise is cheap and constant — a filing, a ratio trend, a management transcript is not. The discipline is going back to what a company actually reported and reasoning forward from there, rather than backward from a price move or a narrative that's already popular.

Writing clarity

If a thesis can't be written down plainly — what's known, what's calculated, and what's being assumed — it isn't ready to be acted on. Writing is where a fuzzy hunch either survives contact with the evidence or falls apart. That's why every answer here separates fact from inference instead of blending them into one confident-sounding paragraph.
