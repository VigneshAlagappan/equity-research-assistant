About Signals

A personal research desk.

Signals pulls a company's financials, filings, commentary and relevant outside data into one place, and lets you ask questions against that evidence instead of against a headline. It works like a research analyst on your side. It reads primary sources, connects the evidence, shows how it got there, and says so when an answer stops being fact and turns into interpretation.

Why Signals exists

Our mission: to bring clarity to financial decisions.

Our vision: to give every investor an equal chance at a good financial decision.

An equal chance doesn't mean everyone should decide the same way or end up in the same place. Investors have different goals, risk tolerances, time horizons and ideas of success. It means the quality of information, evidence and analysis available to an individual investor shouldn't depend on whether they have an institutional research desk behind them. Signals is built to close that gap, so investors can see the evidence, understand what it means, try different questions, and form their own view.

How it works

Everything starts with the sources. Every number on Signals traces back to a source document and a reconciliation decision: annual reports, exchange filings, regulatory disclosures and licensed data exports, cross-checked where that makes sense instead of taken on faith from a single vendor.

On top of that evidence sits a research assistant you can put real questions to. Analyze a company over ten years. Compare two businesses. Dig into why a margin moved. Look at how macroeconomic conditions relate to company performance, or follow a broader economic shift until it shows up in one balance sheet.

Because the goal is to make the reasoning visible, each claim the assistant makes carries a label. A FACT comes straight from a source. A CALCULATION is derived from the underlying data. An INFERENCE is the model reasoning beyond what was reported. That labeling is central to the product, and it connects directly to how we think investing should work.

An investment philosophy

Signals starts from a belief: good investing decisions come from clarity, not from conviction borrowed from someone else's headline. That clarity has two parts.

Thinking clarity

Form the view yourself, from evidence, before someone else's opinion becomes your starting point. Noise is cheap and constant. A headline, a target price or a popular narrative can travel around the world in minutes. Primary evidence often says something different: a filing, a ratio trend, a management transcript, a shift in capital allocation, a long-run relationship between the economy and a business.

The discipline is to go back to what the company actually reported, understand the context, and reason forward from there, instead of reasoning backward from a price move or a story everyone already likes. Signals is meant to make that easier. It doesn't do the thinking for you.

Writing clarity

If you can't write a thesis down plainly, it probably isn't ready to act on. That means saying what is known, what was calculated, what is assumed, and what would make it wrong. Writing forces precision. It's where a fuzzy hunch either holds up against the evidence or falls apart.

That's why Signals keeps fact, calculation and inference separate instead of blending them into one confident paragraph. Confidence should come from how strong the evidence is, not from the tone of the answer.

The investor stays in control

Signals won't tell you what to buy or sell. It helps you investigate. Two investors can look at the same evidence and reasonably disagree. One weighs growth, another resilience, cash generation, valuation or downside protection. Where you end up, and how you get there, is your call. Signals just makes the landscape easier to see.

See the evidence. Understand the reasoning. Make the decision your own.
