Methodology & assumptions

Signals investigations run a hypothesis-driven pipeline, not a single-pass answer. Generate — several competing, falsifiable hypotheses are proposed before any evidence is gathered, spanning financial, operational, competitive, strategic, management, regulatory, macro, and industry angles, each broken into a short causal chain (cause → observed effect). Gather & evaluate — each hypothesis is checked independently against retrieved evidence (financial facts, filings, knowledge-graph relationships), with up to one additional targeted retrieval pass if the first evaluation reports a specific evidence gap. Synthesize — hypotheses are ranked and the strongest explanation is written up, alongside what remains unanswered.

  • Supported / Partially supported / Refuted / Insufficient evidence is an independent verdict per hypothesis, not a single overall confidence score for the investigation.
  • The Leading / Contested / Weak / Unscored badge and percentage on each hypothesis is the evaluator's own 0-100 estimate of how strongly the evidence IT reviewed supports that hypothesis's verdict — Leading is 60% or higher, Contested is 30-59%, Weak is below 30%, and Unscored means no estimate was returned (including every hypothesis evaluated before this scoring existed). It is not a probability the hypothesis is true, and scores aren't comparable across different investigations, since each one's evidence base differs.
  • The hypothesis chain is the same causal mechanism the hypothesis was generated with, broken into short stages by the model at generation time (Step 2E) — it states how the hypothesis claims things would work, not a verified sequence of events; whether it actually held is what the verdict and evidence checklist below it address.
